Human Population Impact
on the Environment

How do human population growth, resource use, and everyday choices affect Earth's ecosystems? Explore evidence, analyze environmental changes, and consider actions that can reduce our impact.

4-part web lessonVideos + interactive dataEstimated time: 2–3 class periods

Before you begin

Did you impact the environment today? Did you travel in a vehicle, turn on a light, throw something away, use water, or eat packaged food? Human activities use resources and can change ecosystems. In this lesson, you will investigate both the effects of a growing human population and the effects of how people use resources.

Learning targets
  • I can describe how human population growth and resource consumption affect Earth's limited resources.
  • I can explain how human activities can change populations and energy flow in ecosystems.
  • I can interpret environmental data and use evidence to support a conclusion.
  • I can identify realistic actions that may reduce negative environmental impacts.

Part A

The Growing Human Population

Earth has a limited ability to provide the resources people need. Population size changes over time, and rapid growth can increase demand for food, water, land, and energy.

Part B

Impacts of the Human Population

Human activities can affect climate, land, water, biodiversity, and the movement of energy through food webs.

▶ Watch: Human Population Impacts – Bozeman Science
Explore WorldMapper maps ↗

Explore how human activities change ecosystems

Human impacts do not stop at resource use. Introducing a nonnative species, breaking habitat into smaller pieces, or releasing pollutants can change which organisms survive, how large populations become, and how energy moves through a food web.

Invasive species

A species introduced to a new area can spread and compete with native organisms, eat them, or change their habitat.

Example: zebra mussels

Habitat fragmentation

Roads, buildings, and farms can divide one large habitat into smaller, separated patches. Populations may become isolated and have less access to food, mates, and shelter.

Example: roads through forests

Pollution

Chemicals or excess nutrients can harm organisms. Some pollutants build up in organisms and become more concentrated at higher trophic levels.

Example: pesticide in a food web

Check your thinking: A population change can cause a ripple effect. For example, if a prey population decreases, predators may have less food. If a predator decreases, some prey populations may increase. The direction and size of the effect depend on the ecosystem.
Part C

Your Ecological Footprint

Your ecological footprint estimates how much biologically productive land and water is needed to support your lifestyle and absorb some of the waste it produces.

If everyone on Earth lived the way you do, how many Earths would be needed to support everyone? Your choices about transportation, food, housing, energy, and purchases can influence your footprint.

🌎 Open the Ecological Footprint Calculator ↗

Complete the calculator. Answer the questions below using your result. If the calculator is unavailable, describe which lifestyle choices you think would increase or decrease a person's footprint.

Remember: The calculator is an estimate, not a grade or a judgment about you. Focus on understanding how everyday choices connect to resource use.
